LinkedIn is the platform that displays Content Credentials most completely: an image or video carrying C2PA information gets a C2PA icon, and clicking it shows the credential's metadata, including whether AI was used, the app or device, the creator, the issuer and the issue date. It does not reject unmarked content. Which trust list it checks, and what it shows for an untrusted signer, is not stated.

- Displays Content Credentials: **yes**, a C2PA icon on image and video, "visible to all viewers".
- Shows: whether AI was used, app or device, creator, **issuer**, issue date.
- Ads: the icon appears on single image or video ads when the media is uploaded.
- Self-attestation: no longer mentioned on its help page (it was until late October 2026); whether it still exists is **UNVERIFIED**.
- Rejects unmarked content: **no**. Trust list and untrusted-signer display: **UNVERIFIED**.

## What LinkedIn says it does

From its Help page, read 29 October 2026: members "can view the Content Credentials on all image and video content that has C2PA information attached". If you post media with credentials, "The icon will be visible to all viewers of your content." The credential panel shows whether AI was used, the app or device used, the creator, the issuer and the issue date; "Issued by" can be an individual, an organisation or a trust authority. For sponsored content, the icon appears on single image or video ads when the media is uploaded, including through Campaign Manager. LinkedIn also says that "it's not yet possible to identify and label all AI-generated and modified content", and that the feature is rolling out gradually.

The page does not say which trust list LinkedIn checks, whether it preserves credentials in files that viewers download, or what it displays when the signer is not on the C2PA Trust List.

## What this means for your output

LinkedIn is where the signer's name is most visible: the panel shows "Issued by", so a manifest signed by a supplier shows the supplier and one signed under your own listing shows you. It is the clearest case for own-name signing among the five platforms, and the one where a company's audience is most likely to click the icon. Whether the icon appears for an untrusted signer, and what the panel says then, is in our test set; this page will record it.
