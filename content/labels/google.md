Google reads C2PA metadata in two places that matter to a company generating images: Search's "About this image" panel, which tells a searcher whether an image was created or edited with AI, and Google Ads' "How this ad was made" disclosure, which does the same for ad creatives. Google's own generative tools embed both a SynthID watermark and C2PA metadata; for creatives made elsewhere, the ad disclosure is the advertiser's declaration. Neither feature rejects unmarked content.

- Search: **"About this image"** shows AI creation or editing when C2PA metadata is present, in Google Images, Lens and Circle to Search.
- Ads: **"How this ad was made"**, in the ad's More menu, "available only for ads that use AI edited or created assets"; automatic for some ads made with Google's products, self-declared otherwise.
- Google's own tools embed **"machine-readable metadata (SynthID and C2PA)"**.
- Rejects unmarked content: **no**.
- Visible on-ad labels by region: **UNVERIFIED** (trade press: EU, India, New York).

## What Google says it does

From Google's post of 17 September 2024, read 12 October 2026: "If an image contains C2PA metadata, people will be able to use our 'About this image' feature to see if it was created or edited with AI tools", a feature "accessible in Google Images, Lens and Circle to Search". On ads: "Our ad systems are starting to integrate C2PA metadata. Our goal is to ramp this up over time and use C2PA signals to inform how we enforce key policies." Google co-wrote version 2.1 of the Content Credentials specification and sits on the C2PA steering committee.

The My Ad Center help page, read the same day, lists "How this ad was made (available only for ads that use AI edited or created assets)" as an option in an ad's More menu, letting a viewer "View information about how the ad was created and whether AI was used to create or edit the ad". Labels come from two sources: "Labels applied automatically by Google to some ads generated by Google's advertising products", and "An advertiser can choose to label and disclose an asset as AI generated or edited". The page's section on trust names "machine-readable metadata (SynthID and C2PA)". Trade press dates the feature's launch to 9 July 2026 and reports visible on-ad labels as mandatory in the EU, India and New York; Google's page says neither, so both are secondary.

Secondary reports from 2026 say Google is adding C2PA verification to the Gemini app first, with Search and Chrome to follow; UNVERIFIED against a Google page.

## What this means for your output

For images, a manifest means a searcher can see your output's AI origin in About this image, with the signer as the manifest records it. For ads, a creative you generated with your own stack needs the advertiser's disclosure switched on; a creative made with Google's tools is labelled automatically. Google's own line of enforcement runs on SynthID and C2PA, so an output carrying both a manifest and a watermark is the shape its systems are built to read.
